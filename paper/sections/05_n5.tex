\section{The five-vertex exact degree and minimum fingerprints}
\label{sec:n5}

On five labelled vertices there are twenty non-loop arcs and $2^{20}$ labelled
patterns.  Canonicalization gives $9608$ $S_5$-orbits and $17{,}824$
$A_5$-orbits.  The symmetric count agrees with McKay's independent
unlabelled-digraph census \cite{McKayDigraphData}; all load-bearing signatures
and minima are checked by the distributed certificate verifier.  The
exact-degree coordinate profiles are $(1,5,16,61)$ and
$(1,6,21,96)$, so the cumulative quartic dictionaries have sizes $83$ and
$124$.

\begin{theorem}[Five-vertex degree and global minimum fingerprints]
\label{thm:n5-minimum}
The signatures through degree three leave $190$ unordered residual pairs for
$S_5$ and $113$ for $A_5$.  The signatures through degree four separate all
$9608$ and $17{,}824$ pattern orbits, so the least separating degree is four
for both actions.  Within the full degree-at-most-four dictionaries, the
exact minimum separating sizes are $14$ of $83$ and $17$ of $124$.
Explicit fingerprints attain those sizes, while complete checked branch DAGs
exclude budgets $13$ and $16$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The nonempty residual-pair lists certify noninjectivity through degree three.
For degree four, independently reconstructed signature matrices have one row
for every canonical pattern orbit and no repeated row.  This proves
injectivity and hence exact degree four.

For the global upper bounds, the checker evaluates all coordinates on all
canonical pattern representatives and verifies injectivity for the following
families.  The canonical coordinate IDs are grouped by degree for readability:

\begin{align*}
&\coord{S5:5:1:00001};\\
&\coord{S5:5:2:00003},\coord{S5:5:2:00011},
 \coord{S5:5:2:00012},\coord{S5:5:2:00022};\\
&\coord{S5:5:3:00016},\coord{S5:5:3:00025},
 \coord{S5:5:3:00148},\coord{S5:5:3:00448};\\
&\coord{S5:5:4:0006a},\coord{S5:5:4:00125},
 \coord{S5:5:4:00145},\coord{S5:5:4:00158},
 \coord{S5:5:4:00164}.
\end{align*}
\textbf{$A_5$, cardinality 17.}
\begin{align*}
&\coord{A5:5:1:00001};\\
&\coord{A5:5:2:00011},\coord{A5:5:2:00012},
 \coord{A5:5:2:00022},\\
&\coord{A5:5:2:00024},\coord{A5:5:2:00028};\\
&\coord{A5:5:3:00016},\coord{A5:5:3:0002a},
 \coord{A5:5:3:0002c},\\
&\coord{A5:5:3:00124},\coord{A5:5:3:00144},
 \coord{A5:5:3:00184},\\
&\coord{A5:5:3:00188};\\
&\coord{A5:5:4:00116},\coord{A5:5:4:00198},
 \coord{A5:5:4:001c4},\coord{A5:5:4:01182}.
\end{align*}
\end{certificateblock}

For the lower bounds, use explicit pair cores of $249$ and $285$ separator
masks.  Each row stores both canonical pattern IDs, not merely a precomputed
mask.  The checker reconstructs the group, coordinate orbits, pattern
representatives, integer evaluations, and every core separator mask.  It then
checks a complete branch DAG rooted at budget $13$ or $16$.  At a branch node,
every possible choice from a named uncovered separator mask appears as a
child after forced singleton propagation.  Each leaf is closed either by a
packing of more pairwise disjoint masks than the remaining budget or by the
uniform maximum-coverage bound.  All children and all leaves are therefore
locally exhaustive.  The $S_5$ DAG has $63{,}589$ nodes and depth $11$; the
$A_5$ DAG has $213{,}648$ nodes and depth $14$.  Induction from the leaves to
the roots proves infeasibility at the two budgets.  Together with the upper
families, this gives minima $14$ and $17$.
\end{proof}

The accepted proof objects distinguish discovery from certification.
Mixed-integer optimization was useful for locating the witnesses and the
pair cores.  Its infeasibility messages are retained only as diagnostics.
The lower-bound authority is the standard-library checker that reconstructs
the mathematics and validates every DAG transition.  A normal run and a run
with Python assertions disabled produce the same scientific verdict.

The two DAGs contain $277{,}237$ nodes in total.  Their canonical uncompressed
JSON representations are about $40$ MB and $146$ MB; deterministic gzip
transport reduces them to about $5$ MB and $19$ MB.  Compression and hashes
make transport manageable, while the checker supplies the semantic proof.
