\section{Problem, exact contribution, and boundaries}
\label{sec:problem}

Fix $n\in\{4,5\}$ and let $G$ be $S_n$ or $A_n$.  The carrier is the set of
loopless directed graphs on the labelled vertex set $[n]$, represented by
Boolean arc masks.  The group acts by relabelling vertices.  For every
$G$-orbit of nonempty squarefree arc supports we use the associated orbit sum
as an integer-valued counting coordinate.  Two finite questions are then
separated:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item what is the least support degree at which the complete cumulative
  dictionary distinguishes every $G$-orbit of graphs;
  \item inside that least-degree separating dictionary, what is the least number of
  coordinates that still distinguishes every orbit?
\end{enumerate}

The second question is global: every coordinate through the exact degree may
be retained or omitted.  It is not the conditional problem in which all
lower-degree coordinates are fixed and only top-degree coordinates are
minimized.  Section~\ref{sec:conditional} treats that distinction explicitly.

Write $\rho(n,G)$ for the least separating degree and $\tau(n,G)$ for the
minimum cardinality inside the full dictionary through degree $\rho(n,G)$.
The central finite statement is the following.

\begin{theorem}[Unified four- and five-vertex reconstruction theorem]
\label{thm:unified}
For
\[
 (n,G)=(4,S_4),(4,A_4),(5,S_5),(5,A_5),
\]
the exact least separating degrees are respectively
\[
  \rho(n,G)=3,3,4,4.
\]
The full dictionaries at those degrees contain respectively
$19,29,83,124$ coordinates, and their minimum separating subfamilies have
cardinalities
\[
  \tau(n,G)=7,11,14,17.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The four-vertex degree and minimum assertions are proved in
Section~\ref{sec:n4}: explicit degree-two collisions give necessity, complete
degree-three signature tables give sufficiency, certified supports attain
$7$ and $11$, and disjoint structural constraints give matching lower bounds.
The five-vertex assertions are proved in Section~\ref{sec:n5}: the residual
degree-three pairs give necessity, the full quartic signatures give
sufficiency, witnesses of sizes $14$ and $17$ give upper bounds, and complete
checked branch DAGs exclude budgets $13$ and $16$.  The four cases use the
same definitions in Sections~\ref{sec:model} and~\ref{sec:hitting-set}.
\end{proof}

The theorem is accompanied by a support-shape statement.  One degree below
exact reconstruction, every residual pair for $S_4$, $A_4$, and $S_5$ has an
individual tree-supported separator.  For $A_5$, exactly eight residual pairs
are tree-blind, and each is the pair of $A_5$-orbits inside one $S_5$-orbit.
Section~\ref{sec:tree-index-two} records the exact finite meaning of that
index-two contrast.

Beyond the four central minima, we classify all four-vertex optima: there are
$10$ for $S_4$ and $896$ for $A_4$.  The conditional cubic $A_4$ optima have
exchange graph $4(K_4\mathbin{\square}Q_3)$ and a free graded $C_2^3$ action,
which extends to an explicit product description of the exchange graph on all
$896$ global minima.  For $A_5$, the antisymmetric channel of the eight blind
pairs has a primitive rank-four integral core and forces a specified split
parent in every separating fingerprint.  These are finite structural
refinements of the same theorem, not extensions to another carrier or vertex
count.

\begin{proofboundary}
The objects are binary, directed, and loopless.  Coordinates
are squarefree orbit sums of nonempty arc supports.  Equivalence is the stated
vertex action, not complementation, transposition, or an external quotient.
The only vertex counts are four and five.  The paper does not infer a formula
for other vertex counts, does not replace the squarefree dictionary by a full
polynomial invariant space, and gives the index-two terminology no physical
interpretation.
\end{proofboundary}

The certified atlas $\CertifiedAtlas$ is part of the evidentiary package.  It contains
canonical registries, collision masks, upper witnesses, lower certificates,
and independent reconstructions.  The paper and atlas have
different jobs: the paper states and explains the mathematics, while the
atlas makes each bounded enumeration and optimization replayable.
