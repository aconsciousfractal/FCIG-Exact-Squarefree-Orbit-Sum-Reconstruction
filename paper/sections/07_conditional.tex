\section{Conditional top-degree extensions versus global minima}
\label{sec:conditional}

Let $d=\rho(n,G)$ and retain the entire lower dictionary
$\Dict^G_{n,\leq d-1}$.  A \emph{conditional top-degree extension} is a set
$H\subseteq\Dict^G_{n,d}$ for which
\[
  \Dict^G_{n,\leq d-1}\cup H
\]
is separating.  Equivalently, $H$ hits only the residual-pair separator masks
left by the complete lower dictionary.  This differs from the global problem,
where lower-degree coordinates may also be omitted.

\begin{proposition}[Exact conditional top-degree extensions]
\label{prop:conditional}
When every lower-degree coordinate is retained, the minimum number of added
top-degree coordinates and the number of optimal subsets are
\[
  (2,2),\ (4,128),\ (4,2),\ (4,200)
\]
for $S_4,A_4,S_5,A_5$, respectively.  For the five-vertex actions, the two and
two hundred optimal subsets form respectively one and fifty orbits under the
recorded external diagnostic transformations.  These quotient counts are not
part of the scientific equivalence relation.  The four conditional minima
are distinct from the global minima $7,11,14,17$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each action, restrict every residual-pair separator mask to the exact
top-degree coordinates.  An exhaustive minimum hitting-set computation gives
the stated minima and complete optimal-subset census.  Independent implementations
reconstruct the residual pairs and separation masks and agree on every
optimal subset.  Direct signature evaluation checks each recorded upper
family; exhaustive exclusion below the minimum supplies the lower side.

For $S_5$ and $A_5$, apply the separately recorded transformations only after
the optimal subsets have been determined.  Their orbits have sizes compatible
with the totals $2$ and $200$, yielding diagnostic counts $1$ and $50$.
Because these transformations are not added to $S_5$ or $A_5$, quotienting
does not change the graph-orbit equivalence or the optimization result.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exchange cubes for the minimum cubic $A_4$ extensions]
\label{prop:a4-exchange-cubes}
Let $\mathcal M$ be the $128$ four-element subsets of the $21$ cubic $A_4$
coordinates that solve the conditional extension problem.  Join
$H,H'\in\mathcal M$ when $|H\mathbin{\triangle}H'|=2$, so that one coordinate
is exchanged.  The resulting six-regular graph is
\[
  4\bigl(K_4\mathbin{\square}Q_3\bigr).
\]
It admits a free action by $C_2^3$ through graph automorphisms, with sixteen
orbits of size eight.  If $t(H)$ is the number of tree-supported coordinates
in $H$, then every orbit contains a unique member $H_*$ with $t(H_*)=1$.
For the displayed three-generator identification of the action with $C_2^3$,
\[
  t(g\mathbin{\cdot}H_*)=1+\operatorname{wt}(g)\qquad(g\in C_2^3).
\]
Consequently each orbit has tree-count enumerator $z(1+z)^3$, and the full
enumerator is
\[
  16z(1+z)^3=16z+48z^2+48z^3+16z^4.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Re-enumeration from the $21$ cubic separator masks rejects every cover of
size at most three and gives exactly the $128$ members of $\mathcal M$.
The certificate labels every member by a component, a $K_4$ vertex, and a
three-bit word, and verifies for every pair that exchange adjacency is
equivalent to adjacency in $K_4\mathbin{\square}Q_3$ within one component.

Three displayed permutations of the $128$ solutions preserve every edge,
square to the identity, and commute.  Their eight products are distinct;
each of the seven nonidentity products has no fixed point.  They therefore
give a free $C_2^3$ action with sixteen eight-element orbits.  Direct
tree-support evaluation supplies, and uniquely determines, the member $H_*$
of tree count one on each orbit; relative to it the three generators change
the three independent Hamming coordinates.  Summing the resulting binomial
orbit enumerators gives the final formula.
\end{proof}

The factor labels and generators give one explicit certified realization;
no canonical factorization is claimed.  The action is defined on the
solution exchange graph.  It is not asserted to arise from one global
permutation of all $21$ cubic coordinate labels and is not identified with
the separate transpose or odd-relabeling diagnostics.  It explains the
conditional minimum landscape without changing either the conditional
minimum four or the global value $\tau(4,A_4)=11$.

\begin{corollary}[Global $A_4$ minimum-exchange graph]
\label{cor:a4-global-exchange}
Join two of the $896$ global minimum $A_4$ fingerprints when their symmetric
difference has size two.  The resulting graph is twelve-regular and is
explicitly isomorphic, via the certified omitted-quadratic and cubic-factor
labels, to
\[
  K_7\mathbin{\square}
  4\bigl(K_4\mathbin{\square}Q_3\bigr)
  \cong
  4\bigl(K_7\mathbin{\square}K_4\mathbin{\square}Q_3\bigr).
\]
It has $896$ vertices, $5{,}376$ edges, and four connected components of size
$224$.  The preceding free $C_2^3$ action extends trivially across the $K_7$
factor, giving $112$ orbits of size eight and global tree-count enumerator
\[
  112z(1+z)^3=112z+336z^2+336z^3+112z^4.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:n4-landscapes}, every global minimum has a unique
form
\[
  \{\ell\}\cup\bigl(\mathcal Q\setminus\{q\}\bigr)\cup H,
  \qquad q\in\mathcal Q,\quad H\in\mathcal M,
\]
where $\ell$ is the forced linear coordinate, $\mathcal Q$ is the set of seven
quadratic coordinates, and $\mathcal M$ is the set of $128$ conditional cubic
minima.  Two such fingerprints differ by one exchange exactly when either
their omitted quadratic coordinates differ and their cubic factors agree, or
their omitted quadratic coordinates agree and their cubic factors are
adjacent in the exchange graph on $\mathcal M$.  If both factors change, the
symmetric difference has size at least four.  Hence the global graph is
$K_7\mathbin{\square}\Gamma(\mathcal M)$, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:a4-exchange-cubes} gives the displayed factorization.
The degree, edge count, and component sizes follow immediately.  Acting only
on $H$ gives seven copies of each of the sixteen free cubic orbits, and
multiplying the cubic orbit enumerator by seven gives the stated polynomial.
\end{proof}

The resulting total fingerprint sizes are $8,12,26,32$ because the retained
lower dictionaries contain $6,8,22,28$ coordinates.  These totals can exceed
the global minima substantially: in the global problem the optimizer may
discard lower-degree coordinates whose separating work is replicated by the
chosen top-degree family.  In particular, the former greedy bounds $8$ and
$12$ for the four-vertex actions become exact only for this conditional
problem, not for the global $19$- and $29$-coordinate hitting sets.
