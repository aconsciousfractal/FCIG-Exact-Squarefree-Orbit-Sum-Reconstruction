\section{Conditional top-degree extensions versus global minima}
\label{sec:conditional}

Let $d=\rho(n,G)$ and retain the entire lower dictionary
$\Dict^G_{n,\leq d-1}$.  A \emph{conditional top-degree extension} is a set
$H\subseteq\Dict^G_{n,d}$ for which
\[
  \Dict^G_{n,\leq d-1}\cup H
\]
is separating.  Equivalently, $H$ hits only the residual-pair separator masks
left by the complete lower dictionary.  This differs from the global problem,
where lower-degree coordinates may also be omitted.

\begin{proposition}[Exact conditional top-degree extensions]
\label{prop:conditional}
When every lower-degree coordinate is retained, the minimum number of added
top-degree coordinates and the number of optimal subsets are
\[
  (2,2),\ (4,128),\ (4,2),\ (4,200)
\]
for $S_4,A_4,S_5,A_5$, respectively.  For the five-vertex actions, the two and
two hundred optimal subsets form respectively one and fifty orbits under the
recorded external diagnostic transformations.  These quotient counts are not
part of the scientific equivalence relation.  The four conditional minima
are distinct from the global minima $7,11,14,17$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each action, restrict every residual-pair separator mask to the exact
top-degree coordinates.  An exhaustive minimum hitting-set computation gives
the stated minima and complete optimal-subset census.  Independent implementations
reconstruct the residual pairs and separation masks and agree on every
optimal subset.  Direct signature evaluation checks each recorded upper
family; exhaustive exclusion below the minimum supplies the lower side.

For $S_5$ and $A_5$, apply the separately recorded transformations only after
the optimal subsets have been determined.  Their orbits have sizes compatible
with the totals $2$ and $200$, yielding diagnostic counts $1$ and $50$.
Because these transformations are not added to $S_5$ or $A_5$, quotienting
does not change the graph-orbit equivalence or the optimization result.
\end{proof}

The resulting total fingerprint sizes are $8,12,26,32$ because the retained
lower dictionaries contain $6,8,22,28$ coordinates.  These totals can exceed
the global minima substantially: in the global problem the optimizer may
discard lower-degree coordinates whose separating work is replicated by the
chosen top-degree family.  In particular, the former greedy bounds $8$ and
$12$ for the four-vertex actions become exact only for this conditional
problem, not for the global $19$- and $29$-coordinate hitting sets.
