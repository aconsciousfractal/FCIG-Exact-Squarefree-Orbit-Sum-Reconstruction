\section{Tree separators and the bounded index-two contrast}
\label{sec:tree-index-two}

A top-degree support has size $n-1$: cubic for $n=4$ and quartic for $n=5$.
Call such a directed support a \emph{tree support} if the simple undirected
graph obtained by forgetting arc orientations is a tree on all $n$ vertices.
This is a property of the support orbit and hence defines a subset of the
top-degree coordinate dictionary.

For $A_n\triangleleft S_n$ of index two, an $S_n$-orbit is either one
$A_n$-orbit or the disjoint union of two $A_n$-orbits.  In the second case we
call the resulting pair an \emph{index-two split pair}.  This is purely a
finite group-action definition.

\begin{proposition}[Tree and index-two contrast in the finite panel]
\label{prop:tree-index-two}
Every one of the $39$ $S_4$ and $51$ $A_4$ residual pairs at degree two, and
every one of the $190$ $S_5$ residual pairs at degree three, has an individual
tree-supported separator at the exact top degree.  For $A_5$, $105$ of the
$113$ degree-three residual pairs have such a separator.  The remaining eight
pairs are tree-blind, and each of those eight is an index-two split pair of
$A_5$-orbits contained in one $S_5$-orbit.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every residual pair, compute its separator mask inside the exact
top-degree dictionary and intersect that mask with the coordinates whose
canonical supports satisfy the tree test.  The intersections have the stated
nonempty/empty census.  This uses all $8,12,27,41$ tree-supported top-degree
coordinates for $S_4,A_4,S_5,A_5$, respectively.

Independently, construct the map from each $A_n$ pattern orbit to the
containing $S_n$ orbit.  A fibre of size two is an index-two split.  There are
nine split residual pairs for $A_4$ and fifty-four for $A_5$.  Every $A_4$
split pair is tree-separated.  In the $A_5$ residual list, all eight empty
tree intersections occur among the fifty-four split pairs; exact pattern IDs
and separator masks are stored in the atlas.  Rebuilding the full payload by
a second implementation gives byte-identical canonical data, including this
crosswalk.
\end{proof}

The converse implication is false in the finite data: forty-six of the
fifty-four $A_5$ split residual pairs do have a tree separator.  Thus
index-two splitting is necessary for the observed tree blindness but does not
by itself force it.  This distinction prevents the eight exceptions from
being inflated into a general statement about alternating actions.

\subsection{A common tree cover for the symmetric five-vertex action}

The proposition concerns one separator per residual pair.  A stronger finite
optimization asks for a single family of tree coordinates that hits all
$190$ $S_5$ residual pairs.  Its exact minimum is five, and there are $88$
minimum tree-only families in the canonical coordinate order.  For $A_5$ no
tree-only family can hit every residual pair because the eight tree-blind
pairs have empty tree separator masks.

When the tree restriction is removed, each of the two minimum conditional
$S_5$ quartic extensions has four coordinates: exactly three have tree
supports and the fourth has an underlying support that is unicyclic on its
touched vertices.  This shape statement belongs to the bounded atlas and is
not used in the proof of the global $14$-coordinate minimum.

\begin{proofboundary}
The words ``split pair'' and ``tree-blind'' refer only to orbit fibres and
directed support shapes in the declared finite model.  They carry no
geometric, stereochemical, or physical assertion.
\end{proofboundary}
